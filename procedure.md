1. Obtain conditioned leather samples sampled in accordance with ISO 2418 from the official testing location on the hide (butt area).
2. Cut two test specimen strips from each leather sample using a precision cutter or steel template, one parallel to the backbone and one perpendicular to the backbone (along lines of toughness), measuring **50.0 ± 2.0 mm** in length and **2.0 to 3.0 mm** in width.
3. Open the draft shield of the analytical balance, tare the balance to 0.000 g, place the specimen strip on the weighing pan, and record its initial conditioned dry mass ($m_0$) to the nearest 0.001 g.
4. Using precision punch pliers or a circular punch, make a small hole (Ø 1.0 to 1.5 mm) at each end of the strip, approximately 3.0 mm from the cut edge, ensuring the holes are centered without tearing the strip boundaries.
5. Place a 500 mL tall-form borosilicate glass beaker on the center of the magnetic stirrer hotplate platform.
6. Pour approximately 350 mL to 400 mL of distilled or deionized water at ambient room temperature (20 ± 2 °C) into the beaker.
7. Gently place a clean PTFE-coated magnetic stir bar (flea) into the beaker.
8. Secure the lower hole of the leather specimen onto the lower fixed hook of the testing frame, and connect the upper hole to the flexible suspension thread.
9. Guide the suspension thread over the low-friction jeweled pulley, attaching the standardized counterweight (3.0 ± 0.5 g) and aligning the indicator pointer with the zero graduation mark on the scale.
10. Lower the frame assembly into the beaker so that the entire leather specimen strip is completely submerged in water without touching the beaker walls or the rotating stir bar.
11. Mount the precision thermometer (or RTD probe) in the clamp, positioning the sensing bulb directly adjacent to the vertical midpoint of the specimen strip.
12. Switch on the magnetic stirrer at a moderate speed to create a smooth, gentle circulation without creating vortex bubbles or mechanically agitating the specimen.
13. Switch on the heating element and adjust the heat regulator to achieve a uniform heating rate of **2.0 ± 0.2 °C/min** (initial heating up to approximately 10 °C below expected $T_s$ may proceed at up to 5 °C/min, followed by strict 2.0 °C/min ramping).
14. Observe the thermometer reading and the pointer on the graduated scale continuously as the temperature rises.
15. At the exact moment the pointer begins to move continuously and steadily away from zero (indicating sudden specimen contraction caused by collagen denaturation), immediately read and record the thermometer temperature to the nearest 0.5 °C. This value is the Shrinkage Temperature ($T_s$).
16. For chrome-tanned leather, if the bath reaches 100.0 °C without contraction, maintain at 100.0 °C (boiling water) for 2 minutes to confirm boiling resistance (boil test compliant: $T_s > 100.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
17. Remove the specimen, measure the final contracted length ($L_f$), calculate the percentage shrinkage ($\%S$), compute the average shrinkage temperature between duplicate specimens, and record the findings in the observation table.
